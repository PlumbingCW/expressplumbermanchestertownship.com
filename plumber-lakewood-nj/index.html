Dense older housing and a large rental share

The Census 2000 tables in the township master plan put Lakewood's median home year at 1974. About 27 percent of homes went up in the 1970s and about 20 percent in the 1960s. Homes from those decades may still have original supply lines and fixtures, so check under sinks and around the heater for slow leaks.

The Census also lists the owner-occupied rate at 51.5 percent. That means nearly half of Lakewood households rent. If you rent, tell your landlord or manager about the leak first, take photos, and shut off the fixture valve. If you manage rentals, we can handle after-hours calls through commercial plumbing service.

Closely built blocks and multi-unit buildings often share drain stacks. One clog can show up in a neighbor's tub or floor drain. Tell dispatch which unit and floor you are on, because it changes where a drain tech starts looking.

Signs a Lakewood home needs a plumber today

  • Water stain spreading on a ceilingA leak above is active. Shut off water to the unit and call right away.
  • Gurgling from a floor drain or tubAir is fighting a blockage in the line. Stop running water and call.
  • Rust-colored or cloudy hot waterThe heater tank may be failing. See water heater repair options.
  • Sewage smell in the basementA main line may be blocked. Do not use drains and call a plumber.

If you smell gas

A gas smell is not a plumbing call. Leave the building, then call 911 or the gas utility from outside. Plumbers can help after the gas company says it is safe.

What to do in the first ten minutes of a leak

  1. Find the shutoffTurn the main water valve clockwise. It is usually near the meter or where the line enters the house.
  2. Cut power near waterIf water reaches outlets or appliances, switch off that breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  3. Clear the areaMove rugs, boxes and electronics away from the wet floor, and open a drain or tub to let trapped water out.
  4. Photograph and callTake photos for your insurer or landlord, then call (609) 547-1417 and say which unit and cross street.

Roads, lakes and storm drains around town

Lakewood is cut by Route 9, Route 70, Route 88, County Line Road, Cedar Bridge Avenue and New Hampshire Avenue. From Manchester, Route 70 is the main way in. When you call, give the cross street or nearest landmark, such as Georgian Court University on Lakewood Avenue, Ocean County Park on Route 88, or Downtown Lakewood near the Strand Theater. It helps dispatch place you on the map.

The township calls its soils sandy and well drained, and its stormwater page warns that yard waste can clog storm drains and cause neighborhood flooding. If water is pooling in your basement after rain, check whether the street drain is blocked before you assume a pipe has failed. If the water keeps coming, a failed pump may be the culprit, which is where sump pump repair comes in.

Drain and heater jobs that Lakewood calls about

Clogs are the everyday call in a town with this many kitchens and baths. Slow sinks and tubs are handled with drain cleaning, and repeat backups may need hydro jetting to clear the line walls. A main line that keeps backing up can call for a camera look through sewer inspection.

Water heaters in the older housing stock fail with little warning. When a tank leaks at the base, replacement is usually the answer, and water heater replacement covers it. You hear a clear price before work starts, whichever fix you need.
