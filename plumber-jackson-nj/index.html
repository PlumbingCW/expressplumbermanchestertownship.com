Water and sewer in Jackson: who handles what

Most Jackson homes get water from the Jackson Township Municipal Utilities Authority, which runs about 211 miles of main and 11 production wells. The authority's latest water quality report shows hardness averaging 86 ppm, roughly 5 grains per gallon. That is moderate. It is enough to leave sediment in a tank water heater over the years, so a flush or an anode check keeps it healthy.

The same authority runs the township sewer collection system, with 133 miles of gravity main and 27 pump stations. Most flow goes to the Ocean County Utilities Authority plant in the north. If sewage backs up at several drains, the blockage could be in your line or in the authority's main, so report it to both. Our drain cleaning service can tell you where the stoppage sits by checking the cleanout.

One more detail matters if you live near Six Flags in the western corner. That area has its own separate water system, so check your bill for the supplier name before you call the wrong office.

Newer subdivisions and older cores: what the housing mix means

The township's 2017 housing plan put Jackson's median year of construction at 1988, ten years newer than the county median of 1978. Population tripled between 1960 and 1970, and the 2010 Census counted 20,342 homes. A house from the 1960s or 70s is now past 50, and its water heater has probably been replaced once or twice.

In a 1988 house, expect copper supply lines and plastic drains, which are sturdy but not immortal. Water heaters installed when the house was new are usually near the end of life. If yours is over a decade old and leaking at the base, a water heater replacement usually beats another repair. For houses with chronic low pressure or pinhole leaks, repiping is worth pricing.

New construction continues. The township approved Jackson Woods for up to 1,541 homes in phases. New homes have fewer old-pipe issues, but fixtures and shutoffs still fail, and we handle those calls the same way.

Permits and inspections in Jackson

Plumbing work such as a water heater swap or a new line is covered by New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code plumbing subcode. In Jackson, the Building Department at 65 Don Connor Boulevard handles construction permits, and the township's own monthly reports list plumbing permits and inspections. Inspections are scheduled through the township's online portal and run from 7 AM to 3 PM.

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Building Department before you start. A plumber who does the work should tell you up front whether your job needs a permit. For larger projects such as new plumbing installation, plan inspection time into your schedule.
