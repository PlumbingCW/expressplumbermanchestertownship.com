How our plumbing work is organized

People rarely call a plumber with the right service name. They call with a smell, a sound or a wet spot. This page sorts the work into four groups so you can match your symptom to the right page, then call when you are ready.

Every service is open for 24/7 emergency calls where it makes sense, and planned work is booked for same-day appointments when available. You hear a clear price before work starts. If a problem turns out to be bigger than first described, the plumber stops and explains it before anything else is done.

Manchester Township homes lean old. The median home was built in 1979, and about 52 percent were built before 1980. That means water heater swaps, drain cleaning, valve replacement and sewer inspection make up a large share of the work in the township.

How the work is regulated

Plumbing in New Jersey falls under the standards in New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code plumbing subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15), and the municipal construction office issues permits and inspections for most alterations. Ask when you call whether your job needs one.

Symptoms and where they point

  • Gurgling from a drainAir is being pulled past a partial blockage. Start with drain cleaning and watch other fixtures.
  • Rusty or orange waterIron staining or old pipe. Western-system homes in Whiting can see iron, so mention it on the call.
  • Hot water runs out fastSediment in the tank or a failing element. Look at water heater repair first.
  • Wet spot on a ceilingA supply or drain line above. Close the main valve and call for leak detection.
  • Sump pump runs nonstopFloat switch, check valve or high groundwater. See sump pump repair.
  • Toilet runs on its ownA worn flapper or fill valve. Toilet repair is quick and saves water.
