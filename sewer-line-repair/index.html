Emergency Sewer Line Repair in Manchester Township, NJ

Sewer line repair fixes a cracked, rooted, offset or collapsed pipe between your house and the sewer or septic system, by lining the pipe or replacing a section.

Call (609) 547-1417

Sewer backup: what to do first

A damaged sewer line shows up as backups that cabling does not fix. When sewage comes up in a tub, floor drain or toilet, treat it as urgent. We take sewer line calls 24/7 in Manchester Township, with same-day visits when the schedule allows.

  1. Stop all water use, including the washer, dishwasher and showers.
  2. Do not flush. Keep children and pets out of the affected room.
  3. Wear gloves and boots if you must clean up, and keep wastewater off bare skin.
  4. Take photos for your records and your insurer.
  5. Call (609) 547-1417 and tell dispatch if you have sewer or septic.

Not sure it is the sewer line?

A single clogged fixture is usually a drain problem and is covered on the drain cleaning page. If every drain in the house is slow or backing up together, the problem is likely in the main line.

Signs of a damaged sewer line

  • Backups that keep returningCabling gives relief for weeks, then the stoppage is back.
  • Sewage odor in the yard or basementGas or wastewater escaping from a cracked pipe.
  • Soggy or sunken yard spotsA wet patch or dip along the line path suggests a leak or collapse.
  • Gurgling in several fixturesAir is being forced through a restricted main.
  • Slow drains on the lowest floor firstWastewater tries to rise at the lowest opening when the line is blocked.
  • Rodents or pestsCracked clay or fiber pipe can give them a route in.

The standard behind lining

Cured-in-place pipe lining is described in ASTM F1216, and sewer repairs and replacements fall under New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code plumbing subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15), usually with a permit and inspection. Open the camera findings with the technician before choosing a method.

A camera inspection decides which of these is possible. We will tell you if lining does not suit your pipe.

Trenchless lining is attractive because it avoids a trench, but it is not right for every pipe. If the pipe has collapsed, has a major offset or holds standing water in a low spot, the liner will not fix the shape. In that case a dig or a replacement is the honest recommendation, and we say so.

Why homes here often end up needing a camera first

Manchester Township's median home was built in 1979, and about 52 percent of the housing stock is pre-1980. Sewer laterals from the 1960s and 1970s may be cast iron, clay or bituminous fiber pipe, and each fails differently. Cast iron scales and cracks. Fiber pipe can deform and collapse. A camera tells you which you have before you commit to a repair method.

Pinelands soil is sandy and drains fast, which means the ground around a pipe can shift as roots grow and water moves. Roots push into joints and lift pipe sections out of line. That is why a repeated backup in an older house deserves a scope rather than a third cable.

Ask the township's municipal construction office how permits work for your repair, because sewer work often needs one. Some homes are on septic, where the line runs to a tank and not a municipal main. Tell us which you have before we assume the line ends at the street.

What drives the cost of a sewer repair

  • MethodLining, bursting, a spot dig and full replacement are very different jobs.
  • Length and depthA short shallow section costs less to reach than a long, deep run.
  • What is above the pipeLawn is simpler than a driveway, a patio or a tree root zone.
  • Pipe conditionCollapsed or offset pipe limits the options and changes the scope.
  • Permits and inspectionMunicipal permits and inspections add steps that affect the schedule.

Insurance often treats a sewer line problem differently from a sudden burst. Read your policy or ask your agent what applies, and keep the camera footage and invoice.

How a sewer repair job moves

  1. Emergency stopWater use stops. If sewage is flooding, the tech clears the line first to get you back in service.
  2. Camera inspectionThe scope shows the break, the pipe type and whether lining is possible.
  3. Options and priceYou hear the choices, what each costs in plain terms and what each cannot do.
  4. RepairThe chosen method is carried out, with permits when required.
  5. Final checkThe line is run and often scoped again to confirm the fix.
