Tankless Water Heater Service in Manchester Township, NJ

Tankless water heater service covers installing, repairing and descaling on-demand units that heat water as it flows. It is a planned job in most cases.

Call (609) 547-1417

Tankless basics before you decide

A tankless heater fires only when you open a hot tap, so it never holds a tank of stored hot water. Gas models use a burner and a heat exchanger. Electric models use heavy elements and need a large circuit.

The payoff is a steady stream of hot water as long as the flow rate fits the unit. The trade-off is a higher install cost and yearly maintenance.

Tankless versus tank
PointTanklessTank
Hot water supplyContinuous within the flow limitLimited by tank size
SpaceWall mounted, smallFloor space needed
Install complexityHigher, may need new gas line and ventLower
MaintenanceYearly flush recommendedOccasional tank flush
Upfront costHigherLower

The right fit depends on gas supply, venting and household use.

Signs a tankless unit needs service

  • Error codes on the displayOften flame, ignition, vent or flow issues that a technician can read.
  • Cold burst in a hot showerShort flow, scale on the heat exchanger or a flow sensor problem.
  • Hot water takes longer than beforeScale and mineral buildup reduce heat transfer.
  • Rumbling or kettle noisesScale on the heat exchanger traps bubbles.
  • Weak hot flow onlyClogged inlet screen or a restricted water filter.

If the problem is a stalled flame, check whether the gas supply, vent and condensate drain are clear. A blocked condensate line is one of the simpler causes.

Standards behind tankless units

Gas tankless units are built to ANSI Z21.10.3, and installation, venting and gas piping fall under New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code plumbing subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15) and the fuel gas code. This is why venting and gas line size drive the scope.

Flush and descale: the yearly service

A flush circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger and then rinses it. Most makers call for a flush once a year, with different timing depending on water quality. Skipping it can void some warranties and shorten the life of the unit.

  1. Shut off power or gasThe unit is isolated before work starts.
  2. Connect the flush kitHoses and a pump are linked to the service valves.
  3. Circulate solutionDescaler runs through the exchanger for a set time.
  4. Rinse and clean the filterFresh water clears the loop and the inlet screen is cleaned.
  5. Restart and testWe confirm flame, temperature and no leaks.

What Manchester Township water means for tankless

The two municipal systems here measure about 3 to 6 grains per gallon, which is soft to moderately soft. That is gentler than hard-water areas, but a tankless heat exchanger is a narrow passage, so even mild minerals build up over time. A yearly flush is a sensible schedule here.

In parts of the western system, iron has tested up to about 1 part per million in 2025 data. Iron can clog screens and leave deposits in small passages. If your water stains laundry, mention it, and ask about an inlet filter.

Many homes were built before 1980, so check the gas line size and the venting path before choosing a tankless unit. A unit needs more gas than a tank, and old piping may need an upgrade. This is why planning with a technician matters.

What affects tankless service and install cost

  • Repair or flushA flush is a routine job. A failed board or heat exchanger costs more.
  • Gas line sizeA larger gas line may be needed to feed the unit.
  • VentingSpecial venting materials and a route to the outside add cost.
  • Electrical or condensate drainElectric models need a large circuit. Condensing gas units need a drain.
  • Unit capacityLarger flow rates need larger, costlier units.

For a tank-based plan see water heater installation. If your tank leaks today, start with water heater replacement.
