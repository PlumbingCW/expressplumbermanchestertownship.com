Emergency Hydro Jetting in Manchester Township, NJ

Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure water hose to scour the inside of a drain or sewer pipe clean. It is the fix when a cable only buys you a few weeks.

Call (609) 547-1417

Drain keeps backing up after cabling? Here is what jetting does

Hydro jetting sends water through a flexible hose at high pressure, with a nozzle that fires jets forward and backward. The spray cuts grease, packed sludge, scale and fine roots off the pipe wall instead of just poking a hole through the middle. The result is a pipe that is close to its full inside diameter again.

That is why people call about it after a cable has failed once or twice. The symptoms are familiar: a drain that clears and slows again within weeks, a main line that backs up every few months, or a kitchen line that smells even when it drains. We take urgent jetting calls 24/7, and same-day visits are available when the schedule allows.

Where the code applies

Jetting clears pipe and does not change it, so no permit is usually involved. Repairs that follow meet the code standard in New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code plumbing subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15), and camera findings are often described with NASSCO PACP coding.

Signs jetting may be the better fix

  • Repeat clogsThe same line has been cleared more than once in a year.
  • Slow flow all the timeWater drains, but never quickly, even after a cable visit.
  • Grease smellA rotten odor from a kitchen line often means a thick fat layer inside the pipe.
  • Roots in the mainA camera or a past cabling returned root fragments.
  • Main line backs up with heavy useLaundry day or a full house of showers pushes the line past its capacity.

What to do while you wait for a jetting visit

  1. Stop using water in the house, especially the washer and dishwasher.
  2. Keep people and pets away from any standing wastewater.
  3. Note which fixtures back up and in what order.
  4. Find the main cleanout if you can, usually a capped pipe in the basement, crawl space or yard.
  5. Call (609) 547-1417 and describe the history of past cabling.

Do not try to push the clog with chemicals or a rented jetter. Pressure applied to a damaged pipe can make it worse.

How a jetting visit goes

  1. Talk through the historyThe tech asks about past clogs, pipe age and which fixtures are affected.
  2. Locate accessA cleanout is ideal. Without one, a toilet may be pulled or another opening used.
  3. Camera check when neededIf the pipe is old or the cause is unclear, a camera confirms the pipe can take the pressure.
  4. Jet the lineThe nozzle is fed through the line in passes, then pulled back while cleaning.
  5. Test the flowWater is run through the fixtures to confirm the line drains.

Older pipes in Manchester Township: when jetting is and is not right

About half of the township's homes were built before 1980, and the median home dates to 1979. Cast iron lines from that period often have a rough, scaled interior, and jetting cleans that scale well. But a clay or old fiber line with a broken joint or a bellied low spot may not hold pressure, and a camera look first protects the pipe.

Tree roots entering a joint are common in sandy Pinelands soil, where roots reach toward a sewer line for moisture. Jetting can clear root growth, but the roots return if the joint stays open. If the camera shows a cracked joint, the honest answer may be a sewer line repair after the jet. Soft water means scale is a small player here, so grease and roots are the usual targets.

What affects the price of jetting

  • Line length and sizeA short branch is faster than a long run to the street.
  • SeverityA thick grease layer takes more passes than light buildup.
  • AccessA proper cleanout saves time. Pulling a toilet adds labor.
  • Camera workA before and after scope adds a step, and it also shows whether the fix held.
  • Hour of the callNight and weekend work is priced for the hour, and you hear that up front.
