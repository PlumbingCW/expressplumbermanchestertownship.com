Emergency decisions

What to do first when plumbing fails

Most plumbing emergencies come down to one question: is water, sewage or gas going somewhere it should not? If yes, stop the source first and call second. Our 24/7 line is staffed for exactly this moment, and the person who picks up can talk you through the shutoff.

Handle it yourself or call now
What you seeSafe to try firstCall right away when
Slow sink or tubPlunger, clear the stopper of hairMore than one drain is slow, or water gurgles in another fixture
Clogged toiletPlunger, then stop flushingWater rises toward the rim or backs up in the tub
Sewage coming up a floor drainStop all water use, keep people clearAlways. This is an emergency plumber call
Burst or leaking pipeShut off the main water valveWater keeps coming, or a ceiling is sagging or wet
No hot waterCheck the breaker or pilot light onceWater pools under the tank, or it is a cold night
Dripping faucet or running toiletReplace a washer or flapper if you are comfortableThe shutoff valve will not close, or the drip floods a cabinet

Anything involving sewage, a flooding basement or a soaked ceiling should be treated as urgent.

Smell gas? Leave first.

If you smell gas or hear a hiss near an appliance, leave the building and call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Do not flip switches or look for the source. Call us afterward for any plumbing work that follows.

Why the details matter

Local plumbing realities in Manchester Township

Manchester Township covers a lot of ground, and Whiting is the largest community inside it, with about 33,000 residents. County Route 530 runs through Whiting, and Route 70 crosses the township near our Route 70 address. A township this size means you should give dispatch your cross streets, not just a ZIP.

Housing age shapes most of the calls. The median home here was built in 1979, and about 52 percent of the roughly 26,400 homes went up before 1980. Houses from the 1960s and 70s can still have original shutoff valves, old tank water heaters and drain lines with decades of use. If yours is in that group, plan a valve check and a water heater inspection before something fails at night.

Crestwood Village, Pine Ridge at Crestwood, Leisure Village West, Leisure Knoll, Cedar Glen Lakes and Cedar Glen West are active-adult communities inside the township. Communities built in one era tend to share the same aging parts, so a neighbor's water heater or sewer clog is a good hint to check your own. Know where your main shutoff is before you need it, especially if you winter elsewhere.

Pine Lake Park, the township's largest development, sits around man-made Pine Lake. Homes near lakes and streams in low spots can see groundwater rise after long rain, so test a sump pump before storm season. Sandy Pinelands soil drains quickly, but it will not save a basement with a failed pump.

Two water systems, two sets of habits

The Manchester Township Water Utility runs an Eastern and a Western service area. Both are on the soft side, about 3 to 5 grains per gallon in the east and 4 to 6 in the west in 2025 reports. That means mild scale, but tanks still collect sediment over the years, and an annual flush helps.

Iron is the other difference. The western system, which covers the Whiting section southeast of Manchester Boulevard along Station Road, recorded iron readings above the 0.3 ppm staining guideline in some 2025 samples. If you see orange staining on fixtures or laundry, say so on the call, because it changes what we look at. Ask the utility which system your street is on.

Some homes in the township are on public sewer and others are on septic. Tell dispatch which you have, because a backup means something different for each, and it keeps us from chasing the wrong problem. For more on how we cover each part of town, see our service area page.
